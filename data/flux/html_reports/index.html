Quick Summary

Key findings at a glance: carbon fluxes benefit 45× more from disturbance than water fluxes, effect is dose-dependent and robust across 3 disturbance metrics, but potential forest-type confounding requires further investigation.

Methods & Data

Detailed methodology: 164 sites, 5 EFPs (2 carbon + 3 water), 3 disturbance metrics tested with tertile categorization, 8 Random Forest model variants (with/without disturbance), SHAP importance and Delta RMSE analysis pipeline.

Step 1: Categorization

Comparison of three categorization methods (tertiles, equal-width, natural breaks) for disturbance grouping. Selected tertiles for balanced groups and maximum statistical power.

Step 2: SHAP Importance

Analysis of how important disturbance variables are for each EFP. Key finding: 60× more important for carbon than water fluxes, confirming differential feature importance.

View Analysis →
📈

Step 3: Delta RMSE

Do predictions actually improve? Carbon fluxes show 45% improvement (ΔRMSE = -0.45) at high-disturbance sites, water shows 0% improvement. Effect consistent across all 3 disturbance metrics.

View Results →
🔬

Step 4: Dose-Response

Is the improvement dose-dependent? YES. Benefit appears only at high-disturbance sites and disappears at low-disturbance sites, confirming mechanistic signal rather than noise.

Explore Curves →
⚠️

Step 5: IGBP Check

Confounding analysis: Are forests just naturally disturbed? Forests concentrate in high-disturbance areas (ENF: 54.7% in tertile 3 vs SAV: 0%), creating potential forest-type confounding that requires within-biome stratification to resolve.

Conclusions

Summary of findings with confidence levels: carbon > water (CONFIRMED, high confidence), dose-dependent (CONFIRMED, high confidence), IGBP confounding (PLAUSIBLE, medium confidence). Plus recommendations for future work.
